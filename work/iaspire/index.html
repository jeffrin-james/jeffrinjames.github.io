Behavioural System Design & Gamification of iAspire

This project explores how behavioural design principles can be applied to sustain user's motivation over time by designing incentive systems responsibly.

App name / Client
iAspire - Accenture
My Role
UX/Product Designer
Industry
Quick Commerce (B2C)
Platform
Web - Employee Portal
Why this project exists

This project was done earlier in my career and represents a formative exploration into behavioural design and incentive systems. It shaped how I think about motivation, effort, and trust when designing products that aim to change long-term user behaviour. The focus here is not just visual polish, but decision-making and trade-offs.

TL;DR

Designed a behavioural incentive system for an enterprise learning platform, focusing on sustained motivation through responsible rewards, visible progress, and habit-forming mechanics rather than short-term gamification.

1.The problem

Enterprise learning platforms often see strong initial sign-ups but poor long-term engagement. Employees start courses, but drop off once novelty fades.

2.The (real) challenge

The problem wasn’t lack of content. It was lack of sustained motivation without over-relying on shallow rewards that could be gamed or ignored.

3.Design question

How might we design an incentive system that encourages consistent learning without undermining intrinsic motivation or trust?

4.Design principles

Instead of leading with gamification mechanics, I framed the system around three principles:

  • Effort should feel visible — users need to see progress accumulate meaningfully.
  • Rewards should feel earned, not automatic — to avoid manipulation.
  • Motivation should sustain, not spike — short-term dopamine was not the goal.

These principles guided every design decision that followed.

5.Decision 1: Earning mechanics

I explored different ways users could earn value through learning actions, namely:

  • Course completion/progress
  • Consistency over time
  • Depth vs breadth of learning

The decision

I chose an earning model based ob coins, badges and collectibles tied to effort and consistency, not just completion.

Why I took the decision

  • Completion-only rewards encouraged rushing
  • Consistency rewarded habit formation
  • Points provided flexibility without forcing competition

6.Decision 2: Rewards, but with constraints

The risk

Unrestricted rewards can cheapen effort and be exploited.

Design decision

I introduced a curated rewards store with:

  • Limited, rotating rewards
  • Clear effort-to-reward mapping
  • No instant gratification loops

The outcome

This balanced motivation with restraint, ensuring rewards reinforced learning rather than replacing it.

7.Decision 3: Progress visibility

The problem

Users struggled to understand how their earning translated into long-term progress.

Solution

Designed a passbook-style progress view that showed:

  • Accumulated effort
  • Learning history
  • Clear next milestones

Outcome

This reframed learning as a journey rather than a checklist.

8.Decision 4: Social signals

Consideration

Leaderboards can motivate, but also discourage slower learners.

Approach

  • Limited leaderboard exposure
  • Focus on personal progress first
  • Social comparison as optional, not default

Outcome

This avoided anxiety while still enabling light social motivation.

9.Decision 5: System visibility

Motive

To provide all-round view of how the entire system functioned (so that the decisions they take would be informed and directional towards the aspirational goals). I utilised the Profile section of the user for this.

This system worked as a loop:

  • Earn visible progress
  • Redeem meaningful rewards
  • Reinforce habit

Outcome

A behavioural system that reinforced consistent learning habits over time, prioritising sustainability over short-term gamification.

10.Impact of the project

Engagement Quality:

Encouraged repeat participation and reduced drop-off after initial course completion, shifting engagement toward more consistent learning behaviour.

Behavioural Shift:

Shifted user behaviour from completion-driven activity to ‘consistent-effort’ based engagement and non-exploitation of system rewards.

Retention Proxy Metrics:

Improved learning continuity by measuring factors like return frequency, session continuity, and longitudinal participation of the user.

System Health Metric:

Designed an incentive system that balanced motivation with restraint, avoiding reward fatigue and maintaining long-term system sustainability.

Stakeholder / Business Impact:

Provided a reusable behavioural framework that could be applied across learning programs without increasing operational complexity and aligned with the user’s learning objectives.

Impact was assessed through participation patterns, progression depth, and qualitative feedback from internal stakeholders.

11.Reflection & learnings

What this project taught me:

  • Gamification is a design responsibility, not a gimmick
  • Behavioural systems must respect user effort
  • Over-incentivisation can damage trust

These learnings now inform how I design user incentives, dashboards, and growth systems in real-world commerce products.
